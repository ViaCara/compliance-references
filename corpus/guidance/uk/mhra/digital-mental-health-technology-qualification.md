# Digital mental health technology: qualification and classification (MHRA, curated quotes)

_MHRA guidance on when digital mental health technology (DMHT) has a
medical purpose and when it is software as a medical device. Version 1.3,
last updated 29 July 2026. Curated quotes only; the full guidance is the
PDF linked from the source URI. Reviewed 2026-09-11 by kylewelsby._

## Medical purpose (section 6.1.2)

> DMHT are considered to have a medical purpose if they are intended as
> part of the broad process involved in the management of mental ill
> health. This includes DMHTs that: - help with assessing risk,
> diagnosing, predicting, monitoring, treating or preventing mental health
> conditions and / or symptoms; - where the conditions and / or symptoms
> are at levels considered diagnosable or clinically relevant; - and are
> either intended for patients, the public and / or healthcare
> professionals.

The guidance breaks the question into two steps:

> 1) Does the DMHT perform a clinical task?

> 2) Does the DMHT target clinical conditions and symptoms?

Table 1 lists, among its examples of clinical tasks:

> Recommends therapeutic options, clinical decision support

> Psychoeducation, builds knowledge and skills

Among the products it treats as having no medical purpose:

> Products that provide healthcare administration support only (e.g.
> booking an appointment, requesting a prescription).

## Functionality (section 6.2)

High functionality includes:

> E. Processes user instruction with an interactive and / or personalised
> output

> G. Processes data / information using AI

> Medical purpose + low functionality = not SaMD Medical purpose + high
> functionality = SaMD

Worked function 24, rated High:

> AI coach / companion / therapist may give personalised advice and
> suggest user completes certain modules / practices to help particular
> situation.
