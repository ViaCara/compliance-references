# Eligibility for Enhanced with Barred List DBS checks with children (DBS guidance, curated quotes)

_DBS guidance on which roles are eligible for an Enhanced DBS check with a
Children's Barred List check, covering the specified activities,
establishments and positions that count as regulated activity with children
under the Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006, and the roles that are
eligible without being in regulated activity. Published by the Disclosure and
Barring Service on gov.uk; the live page shows Published 1 September 2026,
with a companion page Children's Barred List check legislation of the same
date. It replaces the earlier regulated activity with children guidance in the
DBS eligibility guidance collection. The guidance does not address self-
employed applicants; the Code of Practice glossary and the Update Service
employer guide cover the umbrella body route. Curated quotes only; the full
guidance is at the source URI. Reviewed 2026-09-03 by kylewelsby._

## 1. Introduction

Disclaimer at the head of the page:

> Disclaimer: This is not legal advice. If you need help with making sure you
> are complying with the law, you should speak to a legal adviser.

Purpose and scope, paid or unpaid:

> This guidance is designed to help you determine whether a role is eligible
> for an Enhanced DBS check with a Children’s Barred List check. It outlines
> the specific activities, job roles, and workplaces that may mean a role is
> in regulated activity.  This guidance applies whether roles are paid or
> unpaid.
>
> Some roles may still be eligible for an Enhanced DBS check with a Children’s
> Barred List check even if they do not involve regulated activity. These are
> listed towards the end of the guidance under the section titled ‘Other Roles
> – not regulated activity’.

The fallback when the role is not eligible for the barred list check:

> After reviewing the guidance, if the role is not eligible for an Enhanced
> DBS check with Barred List check, you should then consider whether it is
> eligible for an Enhanced DBS check without access to the Barred List.

## 2. Regulated Activity: Specified Activities

The test for the activities table:

> The below activities, if undertaken often enough to meet the period
> condition are regulated activity with children, meaning the role is eligible
> for an Enhanced with Children’s Barred List DBS check.

Table row Activity cell for the first two rows:

> Teaching, training, or instruction, care for, or supervision of children
>
> Advice or guidance provided wholly or mainly to children about their
> educational, emotional, or physical wellbeing

Table Period condition cell for those two rows:

> On more than 3 days in a 30-day period, or once overnight between 2am and
> 6am with the opportunity for face-to-face contact with children

Table Age of child cell for those two rows:

> Under 18 – but not if the activity is in relation to the child’s paid or
> unpaid employment and they are 16 or 17 years old

Table row for healthcare and personal care (Activity and Period condition
cells):

> Healthcare or personal care (see below for more information)
>
> No requirement - once is enough

## 2.2 Healthcare

Healthcare includes psychotherapy and counselling:

> Individuals who provide health care to children, either as a health care
> professional or under the direction or supervision of a health care
> professional are eligible for an Enhanced with Children’s Barred List DBS
> check.
>
> This includes providing psychotherapy and counselling and also covers first
> aiders where they provide this through an organisation set up specifically
> for the purposes of providing first aid, e.g. St John’s Ambulance and
> community first responders.

## 2.4 Scenarios relating to specified activities

Scenario 3, advice and guidance on emotional wellbeing:

> Nasir works full time for a service set up to support children with their
> emotional wellbeing. Nasir’s role is to speak with children using the
> service and provide guidance to them on how to manage their wellbeing. Nasir
> is eligible for an Enhanced with Children’s Barred List DBS check as he is
> providing guidance mainly to children on their emotional wellbeing.

Scenario 2, supervision of a 16 or 17 year old in their own employment is not
regulated activity:

> Louise is 17 years old and has started an apprenticeship at a local
> construction firm. David is Louise’s colleague and is employed by the
> construction firm, he is responsible for the day-to-day supervision of
> Louise in the workplace. As this supervision is undertaken in relation to
> Louise’s employment David is not eligible for an Enhanced with Barred List
> DBS check. The organisation could ask him to obtain a Basic DBS check should
> they wish to.

## 3. Regulated Activity: Specified establishments

The four criteria for ancillary roles in specified establishments:

> People working in these specified establishments who aren’t carrying out one
> of the regulated activities mentioned above (often ancillary roles e.g.
> cleaners, reception, admin staff) will be carrying out regulated activity
> with children if they meet all 4 of the following criteria:
>
> - They work in the establishments on more than 3 days in a 30-day period, or
> once overnight between 2am and 6am with the opportunity for face-to-face
> contact with the children
>
> - They have the opportunity, because of their job, to have contact with the
> children in the establishment
>
> - They work there for the purpose of the establishment
>
> - It is not temporary or occasional work

## 5. Other roles: not in regulated activity

Roles eligible for the check outside regulated activity:

> The following roles are eligible for Enhanced with Children’s Barred list
> DBS checks in the children’s workforce but are not in regulated activity:
>
> - Individuals who are aged over 16 and living in a household where another
> person in the household has been or is being assessed to carry out regulated
> activity with children and they both live on the premises where that work
> will take place
>
> - Individuals aged 16 and over who live or work in the premises where
> childminding or childcare takes place and have the opportunity for regular
> contact with the children
