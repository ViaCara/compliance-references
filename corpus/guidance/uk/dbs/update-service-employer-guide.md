# DBS Update Service employer guide (DBS guidance, curated quotes)

_DBS guidance for employers and organisations on the Update Service: who may
run a free online status check on an existing Standard or Enhanced
certificate, the conditions that must be met first, what each result means and
how the subscription works. Published by the Disclosure and Barring Service on
gov.uk; the live page shows Updated 28 August 2026. The status check is made
under section 116A of the Police Act 1997 and requires a legal declaration
that the checker may ask an exempted question under section 113A or 113B.
Curated quotes only; the full guidance is at the source URI. Reviewed
2026-09-03 by kylewelsby._

## About the Update Service

What the service is:

> The Update Service is an online subscription service for individuals who
> hold Standard or Enhanced DBS checks that lets organisations and employers
> carry out a free, instant online check to view the status of an existing
> Standard or Enhanced DBS certificate.

Subscriptions are personal to the applicant:

> Update Service subscriptions are personal to the subscriber and must be
> created, maintained, and managed by the subscriber themselves.
>
> If an employer or organisation wants to check the status of an applicant’s
> certificate, they must do so with the individual’s consent, following the
> steps set out in this guide.

A status check is not a standalone safeguard:

> However, it should not be used in isolation. The Update Service will only
> show a change in status when DBS has confirmed that new information exists
> that would be disclosed if a new DBS check of the same level is applied for.

Frequency is risk based, and DBS does not notify the checker:

> DBS won’t inform you if a status changes - you will need to carry out
> periodic checks of a certificate with the individual’s consent to establish
> if changes have occurred.

## When can an organisation check an individual’s status on the Update Service?

The three conditions:

> This can only be done if:
>
> - the organisation/employer has the individual’s consent
>
> - the organisation/employer could legally request a new DBS check for the
> role that the individual is applying for
>
> - it is for the same workforce, where the same type and level of criminal
> record check is required
>
> This is because the organisation is initially responsible for understanding
> and applying the relevant legislation to each role that they are recruiting
> for.

Basic checks are out of scope, and the applicant may show the certificate to
anyone:

> The Update Service is not currently available for Basic checks.
>
> An individual can show their certificate to anyone because it contains their
> information, and they can give consent to an organisation/employer to view
> their status on the Update Service, as mentioned above.

Organisations that are not registered bodies use an umbrella body:

> In some instances, organisations are not registered with DBS and must use an
> organisation that is registered, known as Umbrella Body (UB), to submit
> checks on their behalf.
>
> Where the application is submitted by an UB on behalf of the organisation,
> the UB must understand the role that the application is for, and check that
> the level requested by the employer is appropriate, as they are legally
> responsible for submitting the application to DBS.

Subscription cost and portability:

> For an annual subscription of £16 (free to volunteers) an individual can
> take their certificate with them from role to role (if within the same
> workforce, and where the same type and level of certificate is required).

## Status checking

The legal declaration made before every status check:

> Before you carry out a status check, you will be asked to complete a legal
> declaration confirming that you have the individual’s consent, and that you
> have the legal right to ‘ask the exempted question’. The declaration you
> need to agree with is:
>
> I confirm I have the authority of the individual to which this DBS
> certificate number relates to receive up-to-date information (within the
> meaning of section 116a of the Police Act 1997) in relation to their
> criminal record DBS certificate for the purposes of asking an exempted
> question within the meaning of section 113A of the Police Act 1997; or in
> relation to their enhanced criminal record DBS certificate for the purposes
> of asking an exempted question for a prescribed purpose within the meaning
> of section 113B of the Police Act 1997.

What the declaration asserts:

> By making that declaration, you are saying that you have the right to be
> given that information to make a suitability assessment. You are also saying
> that the information is at the level you are legally entitled to request, in
> relation to the role that you are recruiting for.

Code of Practice and ex-offender policy apply to any recruiting organisation
that accesses the information:

> As a recruiting organisation, if you access certificate information, you
> must comply with the DBS code of practice and must have a policy on the
> recruitment of ex-offenders. This does not apply to private employers
> (individuals e.g. care recipients recruiting someone to care for them),
> however private employers must not share information viewed on
> certificates/the Update Service with any other third parties.

The five questions that must all be answered yes, including sight of the
original paper certificate:

> Before carrying out a status check, you must be able to answer ‘yes’ to the
> following questions. If you answer ‘no’ then you cannot carry out a status
> check:
>
> - At any point since the DBS certificate was issued have you seen the
> applicant’s original paper certificate in person, or if not possible, has
> the employer or recruiting organisation viewed the original paper
> certificate and confirmed that it is genuine? Copies or viewing the
> certificate over video link is not enough. To ensure a certificate is
> genuine, you can check the security features of a DBS certificate .
>
> - Have you checked the applicant’s ID as per the DBS ID checking guidelines
> to confirm their identity, either as part of the initial DBS application or
> before checking the status, if the certificate was not obtained by you?
>
> - Do you have the applicant’s consent?
>
> - Are you legally entitled to the same level of DBS certificate - Standard,
> Enhanced or Enhanced with (relevant) Barred List(s)?
>
> - Does the DBS certificate only contain the exact workforce that you are
> entitled to know about, for the role you are recruiting for?

Workforces and barred lists on the certificate:

> Where the workforce is ‘other’, the police will assess the relevance of
> locally held information in relation specifically to the position being
> applied for. The 3 workforces are child, adult, and other.
>
> If you’re legally entitled to a Barred List check, check that the DBS
> certificate only contains the one(s) you are able to ask for e.g. Children’s
> Barred List, Adults’ Barred List, or both. Some Enhanced DBS certificates
> will show that a check of the Barred List(s) has not been requested by the
> original organisation.

When checks must stop, and the applicant's visibility of every check:

> If a person leaves your organisation, moves to a position where there is no
> legal entitlement to the same DBS check, or withdraws their consent, you
> must stop any further status checks.
>
> If you continue to carry out status checks on their certificate, you are
> breaking the law by accessing data you are not entitled to see.
>
> You should always have the individual’s consent when carrying out a status
> check. All checks are recorded and can be viewed by the individual on their
> Update Service account.

## How to do a status check

Sight of the original certificate and the inputs the service takes:

> You should view the original DBS Certificate and consider any information
> disclosed as part of your suitability assessment. (copies or viewing the
> certificate over video link is not sufficient). If you are legally entitled
> to carry out a status check as per the section above and have the
> individual’s consent, go to the Update Service, read all instructions and
> enter the required information:
>
> - the name of your organisation
>
> - If you are a private employer, so not part of an organisation, please
> enter the phrase ‘private employer’ into the organisation name box.
>
> - your forename and surname
>
> - the details of the DBS certificate being checked
>
> - the DBS certificate number
>
> - the current surname of the DBS certificate holder, as shown on their DBS
> certificate
>
> - the date of birth of the DBS certificate holder, in the format D/M/YYYY,
> as shown on their DBS certificate

No account needed; storing the printed result:

> Status checks are free-of-charge, so you won’t need to create an account.
>
> Once you have successfully carried out a status check you will see the
> result of the check which you can print and then store safely and securely
> in line with:
>
> - the Information Commissioner’s Office employment practices code
>
> - the handling of DBS certificate information policy

## Interpreting status check results

The four possible results:

> “This DBS certificate did not reveal any information and remains current as
> no further information has been identified since its issue.”
>
> “This DBS certificate remains current as no further information has been
> identified since its issue.”
>
> “This DBS certificate is no longer current. Please apply for a new DBS check
> to get the most up-to-date information.”
>
> “The details entered do not match those held on our system. Please check and
> try again.”

Meaning of the no-match result:

> This means either:
>
> - the individual has not subscribed to the Update Service
>
> - the DBS certificate has been removed from the Update Service
>
> - you have not entered the correct information

When information counts as confirmed:

> *Please note: Information is considered confirmed when new conviction or
> caution is positively matched to the certificate, when the individual is
> added to one or both barred lists or when the police confirm that there is
> relevant information that ought to be disclosed.

Status change triggers for enhanced certificates with a barred list check:

> For Enhanced certificates with a check against either of the Barred Lists:
>
> - as above plus if the person becomes barred for either/both Barred Lists

Keeping a copy of the result:

> You can print and keep a copy of status checks, as long as you protect the
> information in line with the DBS code of practice and the General Data
> Protection Regulation (GDPR).

## What should I do if an early confirmation check confirms that my employee has been barred?

The barring offences:

> It is illegal for an employer to knowingly allow someone to carry out
> regulated activity with the group from which they are barred.
>
> It is also an offence for a barred person to work or seek to work in
> regulated activity, within a group from which they are barred.

## Using the Update Service if you are an e-bulk Registered Body

Subscription windows, including the 30-day window from the certificate issue
date:

> If the individual joins the Update Service with their e-reference number,
> the application must be received within 28 days of the subscription being
> initiated.
>
> If an individual subscribes using their DBS certificate number, it must be
> done within 30 calendar days of the ‘date of issue’ on the certificate.

## Examples of how employers can legally use the Update Service

Example 1, the record an HR officer keeps after seeing the certificate in
person:

> Newtown Academy has recruited Imran as a teacher. They checked his ID and
> submitted an Enhanced DBS check in the child workforce with a check of the
> Children’s Barred List. They asked Imran to join the Update Service and link
> his certificate to his subscription. Before he started work, Imran showed
> his DBS certificate in person to the academy’s HR officer. The HR officer
> made a note of the certificate issue date, the type of certificate
> requested, the position applied for, the certificate reference number and
> the details of the recruitment decision taken. The HR officer also obtained
> Imran’s consent to carry out Update Service status checks.

Example 2, an agency with no office that receives the original certificate by
post:

> Lucille decides to switch to a different teaching agency. The new agency
> carries out a remote interview, and Lucille posts her original DBS
> certificate (obtained via the previous agency) to them. She gives her
> consent for them to carry out status checks on the certificate. The agency
> carries out their usual background checks including an identity check, and
> on receipt of the original DBS certificate in the post, they perform a
> status check. They decide Lucille can start work immediately.

Example 5, consent alone is not enough when the checker cannot make the
declaration:

> This is because the legal declaration asks Doris if she is entitled to check
> the children’s list – and she’s not – only the adults’ list. Therefore,
> although Roger has given his consent, Doris cannot make the legal
> declaration required and cannot make an Update Service status check.

Example 6, a status change that turns out to be a bar:

> The new check states that Andrew is barred from working with children in
> regulated activity. Jessica knows that it is illegal for Andrew to offer to
> work, to seek to work or to work in regulated activity when barred from the
> relevant workforce and it would be illegal for her to allow him to do so.
> Jessica withdraws the offer of a place on the course and informs the police
> of this.
