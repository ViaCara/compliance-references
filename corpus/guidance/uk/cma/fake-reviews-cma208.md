# CMA208 - Fake reviews guidance (curated quotes)

_Guidance on the prohibition under paragraph 13 of Schedule 20 to the Digital
Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024. Published by the Competition and
Markets Authority on 4 April 2025. The banned practice applies to commercial
practices from 6 April 2025 (S.I. 2025/272). Curated quotes only; the full
29-page guidance is at the source URI. Reviewed 2026-07-02 by kylewelsby._

## Chapter 4 - Publishing consumer reviews in a misleading way

Paragraph 4.2:

> Publishing consumer reviews in a misleading way is prohibited. Misleading
> publication may occur for example because important information is omitted
> or hard to see, or because of the way something is highlighted.

### Suppressing and cherry-picking reviews

Paragraph 4.3:

> A trader may infringe the law if they suppress genuine negative or positive
> reviews, selectively promote positive or negative reviews or omit
> information around how reviews have been written. These practices might be
> used together or separately.

Paragraph 4.4:

> While it is important to suppress fake negative reviews, suppressing genuine
> negative reviews is problematic. Traders should not:
>
> (a) interfere with the ability and willingness of reviewers to leave
> negative reviews in the first place such as:
>
> (i) through threats of harm or legal action,
>
> (ii) by preventing bona fide users from leaving reviews,
>
> (iii) by arbitrarily stopping and starting review invitations,
>
> (iv) by making an offer of dispute resolution contingent on a consumer not
> leaving a negative review etc.
>
> (b) limit access to and/or the impact of negative reviews by editing,
> withholding or removing such reviews.

Paragraph 4.5 (review gating / cherry-picking):

> Cherry picking positive reviews for publication over negative ones might be
> done either through suppressing negative reviews that have been submitted
> or by encouraging just those who are satisfied to leave reviews. It might
> also involve selecting only favourable reviews to be presented or
> highlighting certain positive reviews when these do not reflect the
> experience being reported by reviewers overall.

## Chapter 5 - Publishing consumer review information in a misleading way

Paragraph 5.1:

> Publishing consumer review information in a misleading way is prohibited.
> This includes circumstances such as where a trader:
>
> (a) displays a star rating or review count that is based on the aggregation
> of a number of reviews, but fails to address the impact of fake reviews,
> for example by failing promptly to update a product's star rating and
> review count where reviews have been identified and removed as fake,
>
> (b) allows consumer review information to be determined or influenced by
> accepting commissions from the trader who is being reviewed in return for
> greater prominence in rankings.

## Statutory anchors

The quoted chapters interpret paragraph 13 of Schedule 20 to the DMCC Act
2024 (see `statute/uk/dmcc-2024/schedule-020.md`): paragraph 13(2)
(publishing consumer reviews, or consumer review information, in a
misleading way) and paragraph 13(5) (definitions). Section 225(4)(c) of the
Act makes every Schedule 20 practice unfair in all circumstances (see
`statute/uk/dmcc-2024/section-225.md`).
