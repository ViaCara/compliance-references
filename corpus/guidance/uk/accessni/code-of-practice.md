# AccessNI Code of Practice (AccessNI guidance, curated quotes)

_The statutory Code of Practice "For Registered Persons and other recipients
of Disclosure Information", published by AccessNI (Department of Justice,
Northern Ireland) on nidirect.gov.uk. The PDF cover is dated October 2020;
the nidirect publication page shows "Date published: 29 May 2018", which
predates the current PDF (uploaded November 2021), so the document's own
cover date is used as the revision. The Code is issued under section 122 of
the Police Act 1997 (Part V as it applies in Northern Ireland), and
Regulation 7(h) of the Police Act 1997 (Criminal Records) (Registration)
Regulations 2007 makes compliance a condition of registration. Superscript
footnote markers and bullet glyphs from the PDF are dropped; bullet items
are rendered as hyphenated lines. Curated quotes only; the full 12-page
Code is at the source URI. Reviewed 2026-09-03 by kylewelsby._

## Statutory footing

Introduction to the Code, page 3:

> AccessNI was established in April 2008 to provide criminal record
> certificates. There are specific legal requirements around the provision
> of such certificates set out in Part V of the Police Act 1997. Section 122
> of that Act requires the Minister of Justice to publish a Code of Practice
> in connection with the use of information provided to, or the discharge of
> any function by Registered Bodies.

## Who the Code applies to

"Who does this code apply to?", page 3. The Code binds Registered Bodies,
including umbrella bodies, and covers information reaching them from AccessNI
or from an applicant:

> This Code of Practice applies to all organisations registered with
> AccessNI under section 120 of the Police Act 1997. These are known as
> Registered Bodies. This includes those Registered Bodies that provide an
> umbrella function to non-registered organisations. The Code covers any
> information provided by AccessNI and made available to the Registered Body
> by AccessNI or an applicant.

Applicants must be told about the Code:

> NB: All applicants for an AccessNI check should be made aware of this Code
> of Practice and provided with a copy on request.

The Code's own scope limit, page 3:

> The Code of Practice does not apply to other third parties.

## Disclosure offences under sections 123 and 124 of the Police Act 1997

Page 4. The circumstances in which a Registered Body holds disclosure
information, and the offence of onward disclosure:

> Although disclosure certificates are provided directly to the applicant,
> Registered Bodies have access to personal information about applicants in
> the following circumstances:-
>
> - in respect of applications submitted for countersigning;
> - where certificates are provided to them by their employees or applicants
> for positions, including volunteers; or
> - from the on-line case tracking function provided by AccessNI through the
> NIDirect website.

> Recipients of disclosure information as set out in the applicant’s
> disclosure certificate must note that it is an offence to disclose
> information contained within an AccessNI certificate:-
>
> - to any person who is not a member, officer or employee of the Registered
> Body or their client (where the Registered Body provides an umbrella
> function) unless a relevant legal exception applies; or
> - to any member, officer or employee where it is not related to that
> employee’s duties.

The only exceptions:

> The only exceptions to this are where:-
>
> - the Registered Body has the written consent of the applicant;
> - the information is provided to a Government Department;
> - the information is provided to any person who has specific legislative
> powers that enable them to see or obtain such information; or
> - the information is provided to a person to whom the Registered Body or
> their client has a statutory obligation to provide such information.

Falsification, page 5:

> It is also an offence to:-
>
> - Knowingly make a false statement for the purpose of obtaining, or
> enabling another person to obtain, a certificate.

## Consequences of a breach

"What happens if the Code is breached?", page 5:

> The Police Act 1997 (Criminal Records) (Registration) Regulations 2007, as
> amended, set out Conditions of Registration (Regulation 7). Regulation 7(h)
> requires Registered Bodies to comply with this Code of Practice.

> Failure to comply with Conditions of Registration can result in refusal by
> AccessNI to issue disclosure certificates, the suspension and/or
> cancellation of registration.

> Failure to comply with requirements set out in the Data Protection Act
> 2018 may also result in enforcement action from the Information
> Commissioner’s Office (ICO).

## Data handling, retention and copies

"Data Handling", pages 7 and 8. Retention is limited to the suitability
decision, and certificate information is sensitive personal data:

> Ensure that where a certificate, or a copy of a certificate, is obtained
> from an applicant that it is not retained longer than is required for the
> specific purpose of taking a decision on that applicant’s suitability.
> (Please note that information disclosed on AccessNI certificates is
> regarded under the Data Protection Act as ‘sensitive personal data’ in
> that it consists of information as to the commission or alleged commission
> of any offence).

An electronic result must not be dressed up as a certificate:

> Ensure that a result received as part of an application submitted
> electronically is not reproduced in such a way that it infers that it is a
> certificate issued by AccessNI.

Umbrella bodies remain responsible for information passed to their clients:

> (For those bodies providing an umbrella function) ensure that any third
> parties they deal with in respect of disclosures are aware of the Data
> Protection Act 2018 and provide them with guidance on secure handling and
> storage of information. For Data Protection purposes, information passed
> to a Registered Body by AccessNI remains the responsibility of the
> Registered Body even if passed to a third party.

A written secure-handling policy is mandatory and must be shown to the
applicant at the point of application:

> AccessNI also requires Registered Bodies to have a written policy on the
> secure handling of information provided by AccessNI and make it available
> to individuals at the point of requesting the person to complete an
> AccessNI application.

> Where AccessNI considers that a Registered Body may be in breach of the
> obligations set out in this section, it may report this to the Information
> Commissioner’s Office (ICO).

## Suitability policy and fair treatment of ex-offenders

"Suitability Policy", pages 8 and 9:

> Have a written policy on the suitability of ex-offenders for employment in
> relevant positions that should not unfairly discriminate on the basis of
> conviction or other information disclosed. This policy should be available
> upon request to potential applicants and, in the case of those carrying
> out an umbrella function, should be made available to their clients.

> Ensure that all applicants for relevant positions or employment are
> notified in advance of the requirement for a criminal record check through
> AccessNI.

> Notify all potential applicants of the possible effect of a criminal
> record history on the recruitment and selection process and any
> recruitment decision.

> Discuss the content of the Disclosure with the applicant before
> withdrawing any offer of employment.

## Eligibility for each level of check

"Eligibility", page 10. The barred-list check needs a position specifically
listed in Regulation 9A or 9B:

> Standard checks – to be eligible for a Standard level AccessNI certificate,
> the position must be included in the Rehabilitation of Offenders
> (Exceptions) (Northern Ireland) Order 1979 (the ROO Exceptions Order).

> Enhanced checks – to be eligible for an Enhanced level AccessNI
> certificate, the position must be included in both the ROO Exceptions
> Order and Regulation 9 in the Police Act 1997 (Criminal Records)
> (Disclosure) Regulations (Northern Ireland) 2008, as amended.

> Enhanced checks with children’s and/or adults’ barred list – to be
> eligible to request a check of the barred lists, the position must be
> specifically listed in Regulation 9A and/or 9B of the Police Act 1997
> (Criminal Records) (Disclosure) Regulations.

> Ensure that before countersigning an AccessNI application to be submitted
> they have assessed the role to be eligible under current legislation,
> correctly requested the correct level of check, and correctly requested
> the appropriate barring list information.

## Annex A: Data Protection Act 2018

Page 12. Criminal offence data needs a lawful basis plus an additional
condition:

> You must have a valid lawful basis in order to process personal data.

> If you are processing criminal conviction data or data about offences you
> need to identify both a lawful basis for general processing and an
> additional condition for processing this type of data.
