# Overview of the Conduct Regulations 2003 (curated quotes)

_HTML publication on GOV.UK by the Fair Work Agency (FWA), within
"Conduct Regulations 2003: guidance for employment agencies and
employment businesses". It summarises the Employment Agencies Act 1973
and the Conduct of Employment Agencies and Employment Businesses
Regulations 2003. Curated quotes cover the definitions, scope, fees,
additional services and the limited-company opt-out; the full
publication is at the source URI. Curated 2026-10-08; review by

## Definitions

> A person who’s looking for or being found work through an employment
> agency or employment business. This includes self-employed people and
> limited-company work-seekers.

(The definition of "Work-seeker" in the glossary.)

> When a work-seeker is introduced to a hirer before any work
> commences.This could be by way of an interview or submission of a
> work-seeker’s CV to a potential hirer.

(The definition of "Introduction" in the glossary.)

## Employment agencies

> An employment agency is defined as a business that provides services –
> whether by the provision of information or otherwise – for the purpose
> of finding workers employment with employers, or supplying employers
> with workers who will be employed by them.

> The Act applies to a wide range of agencies, from recruitment agencies
> to specialist agencies, such as entertainment and modelling agents,
> online platforms and executive search consultants.

## Fees

> Employment agencies and businesses are prohibited from charging fees to
> workers for finding or trying to find them jobs. Exceptions to this are
> for finding jobs for performers and certain other workers in the
> entertainment field, and photographic or fashion models.

> The Act does not regulate the fees charged to hirers by employment
> agencies and businesses, or the rates paid by the employment businesses
> to workers employed by them.

## Additional goods or services

> Under Section 6(1) of the Act, an employment agency or employment
> business must not charge a work-seeker a fee for finding them work,
> unless you’re an employment agency operating in the entertainment and
> modelling sectors.

> You may, however, offer other services that are not related to
> work-finding and you may charge a fee for those.

## Limited-company work-seekers and vulnerable people

> Work‑seekers cannot opt out of the Conduct Regulations where the role
> involves working with vulnerable people, even if they would otherwise
> be entitled to opt out under Regulation 32(9).
